Four quatrains, the rhyme chained so each stanza reaches forward into the next, until the last folds back on itself — *and miles to go before I sleep*, said twice, the second time no longer only about the road. A poem that stops, as its speaker does, on the edge of a stillness it cannot quite allow itself.
