Rossetti sets the Incarnation not in warmth but in the hardest cold — *earth stood hard as iron, water like a stone* — so that the vastness of what arrives is measured against the smallness of the place it arrives in. The famous close turns the whole poem inward: heaven and earth cannot hold him, yet what he asks for is only the heart. Later set to music by Holst and by Darke, but it began as a poem.
