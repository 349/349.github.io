**miniature** *(n. / adj.)* — despite the look of it, **nothing to do with** Latin *minor* or *minimus* "small." It comes from Latin **minium**, "red lead, cinnabar" — the vivid red pigment used to paint the initials and small illustrations of illuminated manuscripts.

From *minium* came the verb **miniāre**, "to colour with red lead," hence "to illuminate a manuscript," and Italian **miniatura**, the illuminator's art; a *miniatore* was the one who did it. Because such paintings happened to be small, the word was quietly reanalysed — by folk etymology — as though it belonged to the *min-* family of **minor**, **minute**, **diminish**, and **minimum**. The sense slid from "painted in red" to simply "very small," and the modern meaning was born of a coincidence: *min-* looked like smallness, and the pictures obliged.

So **miniature** and **minimum** are false cousins. One is a colour; the other is a size. They meet only in the accident of a shared syllable — and in the smallness of a page.
