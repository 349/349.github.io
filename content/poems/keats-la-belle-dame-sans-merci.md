Keats borrows the ballad's old machinery — the question and answer, the repeated refrain, the withered sedge and no birds singing — and turns it on the reader: we are left, like the knight, on the cold hillside, with an enchantment that has already happened and cannot be undone. The title, taken from a medieval French poem, means "the beautiful lady without mercy."
