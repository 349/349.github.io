This is a commonplace book kept in the open — a place to gather the few things worth slow attention and to arrange them with more care than a feed would ever allow.

What holds it together is a single habit of mind: an attention to **form** — to the ordered patterns underneath things, and to the strange fact that order can be beautiful. It is the same instinct at work whether one is following a suspension resolve in a piece of choral music, tracing a word back through three languages to the thing it first meant, or reading how a molecule folds itself into a function. Music, language, liturgy, and the sciences are not separate rooms here; they are the same curiosity pointed in different directions.

So the garden collects a few kinds of thing. **Essays**, for ideas that need room to be argued at length. **Poems** worth keeping, gathered for their own sake and with care for attribution. **Vulgate texts**, read aloud, so the Latin and its sound sit together. A growing set of **etymologies** — small notes on where words have been before they reached us. And **photographs**, organised by subject rather than by date, as a way of paying attention to light, texture, and the shape of built things.

None of this is meant to be complete. It is a garden, not an archive — chosen, tended, and slowly growing. If something here is worth your slow attention, it has done its job.
